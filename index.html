before we start

The Fair Was the Warm Up

A recruiter working a booth for a few hours talks to dozens of students, sometimes over a hundred. By the time they're back at their desk, most of those conversations have already blurred together.

The follow up is the part that actually separates you from everyone else they met, mostly because most students never send one. Recruiters often get asked by their own hiring managers who stood out at the fair, and a good follow up is one of the easiest ways to be the answer to that question.

the main event

The Email That Gets Read

Recruiters are following up with everyone they met too, which means they're skimming. Specificity is what gets you an actual reply instead of a skim and forget.

Subject line, specific enough that they know exactly who you are and where you met:

  • "Great meeting you at [Career Fair Name], [Your Name]"
  • "Following up on our chat about [Team or Role]"
  • "Thanks for the conversation at [Company]'s booth"

The four things every follow up needs, in this order:

  1. Reintroduce yourself

    One line: who you are, and when and where you met. They talked to a lot of people today, so don't assume they remember your face.

  2. Bring back your memory hook

    The specific detail from your notes. This is the single line that proves you weren't just collecting business cards.

  3. Reiterate interest, briefly

    One sentence, naming the role or team if you know it. Skip repeating your whole pitch, they already have your resume.

  4. Make a clear ask

    A real next step, not just "thanks again." Ask about next steps for the role, or that you'd love to stay in touch as they hire.

EXAMPLE
Subject: Great meeting you at the UF Career Fair, [Your Name]

Hello [Recruiter Name],

It was great talking with you at the UF Career Fair yesterday. Thank you for taking the time to come in and speak to all of us. I'm the CS student who mentioned [specific detail, for example working on a computer vision project for my capstone].

I'm really interested in the [Role or Team] you mentioned, especially [reason tied to something they said]. I have recently applied and would love to hear about next steps, or happy to share more about my background if that's useful.

Thanks again for your time,
[Your Name]
[Phone]
[LinkedIn]

Do

  • Keep it under about 150 words
  • Double check the company name and their name
  • Match your tone to the company. A startup booth and a Fortune 500 table read differently

Don't

  • Write a cover letter disguised as an email.
  • Write as if you're send a friend a message.
  • Write it vague enough that it could've gone to anyone

the second touch

Then, Send the LinkedIn Request the Right Way

Send it in the same window as your email, but treat it as a separate touch, not a copy and paste of it.

Skip LinkedIn's default connect message. Recruiters notice that one specifically, and it's an easy way to get lumped in with everyone who didn't bother. Always write your own note.

Keep it to who you are, where you met, and one genuine reason to connect. LinkedIn notes are short, so two sentences is plenty.

Mistakes That Quietly Kill a Follow Up

None of these are dramatic. They're just small enough that people do them without noticing.

  1. 1

    Sending the same templated email to every recruiter. It reads as mass produced the moment two sentences could apply to any company.

  2. 2

    Following up more than twice after your first email too close together. Persistence is good, pressure is not.

  3. 3

    Waiting too long to send anything. A perfect email sent ten days later loses to an average one sent within the week. However always worth sending.

  4. 4

    Misspelling the company or recruiter's name. It's the fastest way to signal you weren't paying attention.

  5. 5

    Only following up with your top choice company. The fair is a numbers game, so give every real conversation a real follow up.

the part everyone skips

If They Go Quiet

Most recruiters won't respond right away. They're working through the same pile of names you are. That's not a rejection, it just means you follow a cadence instead of guessing.

1

Day 0

Thank you email sent, LinkedIn request sent.

2

About a week of silence

One short, polite check in. Reference your first email, restate interest in a sentence, ask if there's any update. No pressure, no guilt tripping.

3

Another week of silence

One last, casual note. It's fine, expected even, for this to be your final attempt.

4

Still nothing

Let the email thread go, but stay connected on LinkedIn. Recruiters move roles, roles reopen, and being known as someone who followed up well is a good reputation to have.

two follow ups after your first email is the sweet spot. it shows you're serious, not spammy.

what if

Common Situations

A few scenarios that don't fit the standard script.

What if I only got a business card, no email or LinkedIn?

Search the recruiter's name plus the company on LinkedIn. Most people are easy to find this way. Send a personalized connection request referencing the fair, and once you're connected you can ask for their email if you still need it.

What if they told me to just apply online?

Apply within a day or two, then still send a short thank you note. Mention that you applied and reference the role, so your application isn't just one of hundreds sitting in the system.

What if I got an automatic no reply response?

That's normal for larger companies. Send your real follow up to the recruiter you actually spoke with instead, using the email or LinkedIn you collected at the booth.

What if I talked to two people at the same booth?

Follow up with both if the conversations were genuinely different. If one person clearly led the conversation, focus your main email there and keep the note to the other person shorter.

What if I forgot to write anything down?

Focus on whatever detail you do remember, even something small like the swag they had out or a question you asked. A slightly generic email sent on time still beats a perfect one you never send.

What if they follow up with me first?

Even better. Reply within a day, answer whatever they asked directly, and treat it as your opening to keep the conversation going.

for later

Pocket Version

  • Connect as soon as possible.
  • Every email needs one specific detail from your actual conversation.
  • Connect on LinkedIn separately, with your own note.
  • No response after a week? Send one follow up. Still nothing after that? Send one more, then let it go and focus on keeping contact through LinkedIn posts.
  • Keep a tracker. It's hard to keep up with what every recruiter said.
